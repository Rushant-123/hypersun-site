Manifesto
The Hypersun manifesto

One terawatt

We're building the world's first intelligent energy company.

I

Energy is the input

Every era of progress has run on more energy than the one before it.

Fire, coal, oil, the grid. Each time we learned to make more power, we learned to make more of everything else: food, medicine, light, knowledge.

The next era is asking for more than any before. AI is turning electricity into intelligence. Factories are coming home. Cities are growing, and the cars and furnaces in them are moving to the grid. Demand is rising faster than we know how to build.

The world is answering with sun, wind, water and atoms, and with grids rebuilt for a new century. The open question is whether we'll build well.

II

We are building blind

Most of the energy system can't see itself.

A solar plant loses a few percent of its output every day and rarely knows where. A string fuse blows and nobody finds it for a week. A grid operator runs on hours-old data. A developer picks a site from a spreadsheet and lives with the answer for thirty years.

The tools that run power today record what happened. They don't know what should have happened, so they can't tell anyone what to do next. The loss is spread across millions of assets, small in each one and enormous in total.

III

Laplace

In 1814, Pierre-Simon Laplace imagined an intelligence that knew the state of everything, and so could see what comes next.

We're building that intelligence for energy. Laplace is a physics twin of every asset, from the panel to the grid. It knows what each one should make in this sunlight, at this temperature, right now. When reality falls short, it finds the gap, prices it, and sends someone to fix it.

Physics is the point. A model that only learns from history repeats it. A model that knows the physics can tell you what a plant should do on a day it's never seen, and what a plant that doesn't exist yet would do if you built it.

IV

How we get there

We'll earn the right to produce energy by first understanding it better than anyone.

That happens in three steps. Each one funds and teaches the next.

  1. SeeTwin every plantOperators already spend billions on control and monitoring software. SCADA alone is a $13 to $15 billion market, and digital twins for energy add another $6 to $7 billion, growing fastest of all. We start there, because that's where the data lives, and we replace hindsight with physics.
  2. KnowSell what the data knowsEvery plant we twin teaches the model. Across many plants and many grids, that becomes something no single operator has: a picture of where energy is lost and where load is going. We sell that insight back to operators and developers, so they know what to fix and where to build next.
  3. BuildMake the energy ourselvesThe company that knows the most about where and how to produce energy should be the one producing it. We will use everything the twin has learned to build and run generation of our own, sited, sized and operated by intelligence from the first day.
V
1 TW

Our goal is to produce one terawatt of energy. That's about three-quarters of the entire United States power fleet, built by one company that can see every watt of it.

VI

An intelligent energy company

There are energy companies, and there are software companies that serve them. We intend to be the first that is both.

Utilities own the assets and buy their software from vendors who have never owned a plant. Neither feels the full cost of a lost megawatt-hour, so neither is built to chase it all the way down.

We will. When the company that models the plant also owns the plant, every insight turns straight into energy, and every megawatt-hour produced sharpens the model. That loop is the company.

VII

What we believe

  1. Physics before opinionEvery decision starts from what the laws of nature say should happen.
  2. Every kilowatt-hour deserves to be seenWhether it was made or lost, we measure it and say why.
  3. Earn trust before controlWe start read-only. We touch a plant only once we've shown we understand it.
  4. Build where the data saysSites, sizes and schedules come from the model.
  5. Abundance is the goalMore energy, cheaper and cleaner, for everyone.
