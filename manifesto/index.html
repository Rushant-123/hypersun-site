The Hypersun manifesto

One terawatt

We're building the world's first intelligent energy company, to produce one terawatt by 2040.

I

Energy is the input

Every machine that thinks will need a power plant behind it.

Data centres used about 485 terawatt-hours of electricity in 2025, as much as all of Germany generates. The IEA expects that to roughly double by 2030. Factories are coming home, and the cars and furnaces in our cities are moving onto the grid. Every era of progress has run on more energy than the one before it, and this one will need more than any.

Making it from sunlight has never been cheaper. Electricity from new solar farms costs nearly 90 percent less than it did in 2010. The world will spend a record $3.4 trillion on energy this year, close to 60 percent of it on electricity, and about a billion dollars a day on solar alone. In 2025, almost 700 gigawatts of new solar came online, taking the total to nearly 3 terawatts.

And the job is nowhere near done. Some 655 million people still have no electricity at all.

II

We are building blind

Almost none of it can see itself.

A solar plant loses output every day and rarely knows where. A string fuse blows and nobody finds it for a week. A wind turbine drifts off its power curve and nobody notices until the quarterly report. A grid operator runs on hours-old data. A developer picks a site from a spreadsheet and lives with the answer for thirty years.

Take solar, the fastest-growing source of all. US sites produce 8.6 percent less electricity, on average, than the estimates they were financed on. If the world's solar fleet does as badly, the output of about 250 gigawatts of panels is being made and then lost without anyone noticing. That's more than the entire grid of almost every country on Earth.

The software meant to catch this is hindsight software. It records what happened, draws it on a screen, and leaves a person to work out what went wrong. It has no idea what should have happened, so it can't tell anyone what to do next.

III

Four ways this goes

As we see it, the buildout can go one of four ways.

  1. Nothing changesCapacity goes up, a slice of every plant's output leaks away, and everyone pays for energy nobody used.
  2. The incumbents bolt AI onto their dashboardsOperators already spend $13 to $15 billion a year on SCADA and another $6 to $7 billion on digital twins for energy. That spend keeps growing, and the plants stay the same.
  3. Every utility and hyperscaler builds its own modelEach one sees up to its own fence line, and nobody sees the system.
  4. Demand winsMore than 2,000 gigawatts of US generation and storage is waiting in line for a grid connection, and the projects that got through in 2025 waited a median of more than five years. Data centres wait, prices climb, and the age of abundance turns into an age of rationing.

We don't want any of these. We think there's a fifth way.

IV

Laplace

In 1814, Pierre-Simon Laplace imagined an intelligence that knew the state of everything, and so could see what comes next.

We're building that intelligence for energy, and then an energy company around it.

Laplace is a physics twin of every asset, from the solar panel, wind turbine, dam or reactor to the grid. It knows what each one should make in this sunlight, this wind and this heat, right now. When reality falls short, it finds the gap, prices it and sends someone to fix it.

Physics matters because a model that only learns from history repeats it. A model that knows the physics can tell you what a plant will do on a day it's never seen, and what a plant that doesn't exist yet would do if you built it.

V

What we believe

  1. Physics over hindsightA plant should be judged against what the laws of nature say it can make.
  2. Every kilowatt-hour deserves to be seenWhether it was made or lost, we measure it and say why.
  3. Trust before controlWe start read-only. We touch a plant only once we've shown we understand it.
  4. Own the outcomeIn the end we'll be paid for energy delivered, so every lost megawatt-hour is ours to find.
  5. Abundance is the goalMore energy, cheaper and cleaner, for everyone on Earth, starting with the people who have none.
VI
1 TW

The goal is one terawatt by 2040, made from sunlight, wind, water, the heat of the Earth and split atoms. That's about three-quarters of the entire United States power fleet, built by one company that can see every watt of it.

VII

An intelligent energy company

Today there are energy companies, and there are software companies that sell to them.

Utilities own the assets and buy their software from vendors who have never owned a plant, so neither feels the full cost of a lost megawatt-hour. When the company that models the plant also owns it, every insight turns straight into energy, and every megawatt-hour it produces sharpens the model. That loop is the company.

So, in short, the plan is:

  1. Twin every plant we can reach.
  2. Sell what the twin learns to the operators and developers who need it.
  3. Use the money and the model to build a terawatt of our own: solar, wind, hydro, geothermal and nuclear fission.

We know how this sounds. No single company has come close to a terawatt, and the odds that a startup gets there by 2040 are small. We're doing it anyway, because the alternative is a century of energy built blind.

The work in between is some of the hardest engineering there is: a physics model of every panel, turbine, reactor and line on a grid, running in real time, trusted enough that people build power plants on its word. If that's the problem you want to spend the next decade on, we want to hear from you.

That's why we're building Hypersun: the world's first intelligent energy company, and the first to produce a terawatt. Build it with us.
