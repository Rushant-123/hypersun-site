The rules

The hardest test in solar

Most forecasting benchmarks let models use data they could never have had in time. Real grids don't. HypersunBench plays by the market's rules, so a score here means money in the real world.

  1. The real market deadlineEach day-ahead forecast is issued at the market's own cut-off on the day before, using only data observed by then. Intraday forecasts are revised at 08:00, 11:00 and 14:00 local time.
  2. No peeking at the futureEvery input a model uses must have existed before the forecast was issued. Nothing measured after the deadline, nothing reconstructed in hindsight.
  3. Plants the model has never seenAbout one plant in five in each market is held out of training. Those plants count in the score and are also reported on their own.
  4. Scored on probabilitiesModels give a range of outcomes for every block of the day, not one number. The score rewards being both close and honest about uncertainty.
  5. Curtailment removedHours when the grid told a plant to turn down are excluded, or scored against the operator's estimate of available power. The benchmark measures weather-driven output.
  6. Recent dataTest windows fall in 2026 wherever the data allows, so the models are scored on months they have not seen. The one exception, the US in 2022, is disclosed.
Markets

Five continents, six grids

From Australia to Brazil, from monsoon India to the English countryside, every plant is scored on the meter data its own grid operator publishes.

The score

One number, every market

One headline score, comparable across markets with very different weather.

Each model gives nine quantiles, from 10% to 90%, for every block of the forecast day. We score them with the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS), which measures how far the predicted range sits from what the plant actually made.

Each plant's CRPS is compared with smart persistence: yesterday's cloudiness carried onto tomorrow's sun path. That baseline is hard to beat on clear days and easy to beat on changing ones, so the score shows how much a model knows about the weather.

skill = 1 − CRPS(model) ÷ CRPS(smart persistence)

Skill is computed per plant and averaged with equal weight, so a 5 MW plant in Britain counts as much as a 500 MW plant in Brazil. Day-ahead and intraday are reported separately.
